\# 06 \- Homepage Content Architecture  
\#\# Version 1.0

\> Status: In Progress  
\> Purpose:  
\> Define the information architecture of the homepage.  
\> This document determines \*what\* visitors should learn, \*when\* they should learn it, and \*why\* each section exists.

\---

\# Core Principle

The homepage is not a biography.

It is not a résumé.

It is not a project gallery.

It is a sales experience.

Every section should answer one question in the visitor's mind.

If a section does not reduce uncertainty or increase trust, it should not exist.

\---

\# Visitor Journey

The homepage should move visitors through five emotional stages.

Curiosity

↓

Understanding

↓

Trust

↓

Confidence

↓

Action

The visitor should never feel overwhelmed.

Every section should naturally prepare them for the next one.

\---

\# Homepage Structure

Hero

↓

Trust Strip

↓

Featured Projects

↓

How I Think

↓

Capabilities

↓

About

↓

Final CTA

\---

\# 1\. Hero

\#\# Visitor Question

Who are you?

Why should I care?

\---

\#\# Goal

Immediately communicate positioning.

Visitors should understand within a few seconds that this is someone who builds software that solves business problems.

Not someone who simply writes code.

\---

\#\# Information

• Positioning Statement

• Supporting Copy

• Primary CTA

• Secondary CTA

Nothing else.

Avoid statistics.

Avoid technology logos.

Avoid social proof.

The hero exists for positioning.

Not proof.

\---

\#\# Emotional Outcome

"I want to learn more."

\---

\# 2\. Trust Strip

\#\# Visitor Question

Can I trust you?

\---

\#\# Goal

Reduce uncertainty immediately.

Trust should appear before projects.

Visitors should feel comfortable investing more attention.

\---

\#\# Information

Examples:

Companies worked with

Years building production software

Products launched

Industries served

Open source contributions (optional)

Only measurable signals.

Avoid meaningless metrics.

\---

\#\# Emotional Outcome

"This person has real experience."

\---

\# 3\. Featured Projects

\#\# Visitor Question

Can you actually do what you claim?

\---

\#\# Goal

Provide proof.

Projects should demonstrate business outcomes.

Not technical achievements.

\---

\#\# Information Structure

Problem

↓

Approach

↓

Outcome

↓

Technology (last)

Technology is supporting information.

Not the headline.

\---

\#\# Emotional Outcome

"They solve real business problems."

\---

\# 4\. How I Think

\#\# Visitor Question

What makes you different?

\---

\#\# Goal

Differentiate through thinking.

Not through technology.

Explain your approach to understanding problems, making decisions, and building systems.

Avoid process diagrams.

Avoid generic development workflows.

Focus on principles.

\---

\#\# Emotional Outcome

"I'd enjoy working with this person."

\---

\# 5\. Capabilities

\#\# Visitor Question

Can you build what I need?

\---

\#\# Goal

Present engineering capability rather than technology lists.

Capabilities communicate value.

Technologies communicate implementation.

\---

\#\# Examples

AI Integration

Backend Architecture

Business Automation

Complex SaaS Platforms

API Design

System Design

Performance Optimisation

Cloud Deployment

Database Architecture

Authentication

Real-time Systems

Payments

Every capability should relate to business outcomes.

\---

\#\# Emotional Outcome

"They can handle complex projects."

\---

\# 6\. About

\#\# Visitor Question

Who are you as a professional?

\---

\#\# Goal

Humanise the portfolio.

This is not a timeline.

This is not a life story.

Explain what drives your work.

Explain why quality matters to you.

Explain why you enjoy solving difficult problems.

Visitors should understand the person behind the work.

\---

\#\# Emotional Outcome

"I trust this person."

\---

\# 7\. Final Call To Action

\#\# Visitor Question

What should I do next?

\---

\#\# Goal

Remove friction.

By this stage the visitor should already feel convinced.

The final section simply provides a clear next step.

\---

\#\# Information

Simple invitation.

Clear CTA.

Professional contact options.

Nothing more.

\---

\#\# Emotional Outcome

"I want to contact him."

\---

\# Information Hierarchy

The homepage should reveal information in this order.

Identity

↓

Credibility

↓

Proof

↓

Thinking

↓

Capability

↓

Personality

↓

Action

Changing this order weakens the experience.

\---

\# Content Rules

Every sentence should do one of three things.

Increase clarity.

Increase trust.

Increase curiosity.

If a sentence does none of these...

Remove it.

\---

\# Things We Never Talk About

The homepage should avoid unnecessary emphasis on:

❌ React

❌ Next.js

❌ MERN

❌ TypeScript

❌ JavaScript

❌ Number of frameworks known

Technology appears naturally inside projects.

Visitors buy outcomes.

Not frameworks.

\---

\# Success Criteria

After viewing the homepage the visitor should believe:

✓ He understands business.

✓ He solves difficult engineering problems.

✓ He communicates clearly.

✓ He pays attention to detail.

✓ He builds products that feel premium.

✓ He is someone I can confidently trust with an important project.

If these six beliefs exist, the homepage has achieved its purpose.
